# Technical Documentation Review Checklist

**Document type:** Editorial and technical review checklist  
**Audience:** Technical writers, maintainers and reviewers

Use this checklist to assess whether a technical document is accurate, usable and appropriate for its audience.

## 1. Purpose and audience
- Is the document's purpose clear?
- Is the intended reader identified?
- Does the content match the reader's knowledge and task?
- Does the opening explain what the reader will be able to do or understand?

## 2. Accuracy and evidence
- Are product names, features, versions and commands accurate?
- Are claims supported by authoritative sources or direct verification?
- Are assumptions and limitations visible?
- Are proposed capabilities clearly distinguished from existing capabilities?
- Are dates and version details included where they matter?

## 3. Structure and navigation
- Are headings descriptive and logically ordered?
- Can readers find key information quickly?
- Are related topics linked?
- Are lists and tables used where they improve comprehension?
- Is repeated information reduced without removing necessary context?

## 4. Instructions and examples
- Are prerequisites stated before the steps?
- Are steps in the correct order?
- Is it clear where each command should be run?
- Are placeholders distinguished from real values?
- Is the expected result explained?
- Are error handling and recovery steps included?
- Have instructions been tested in the stated environment?

## 5. Safety, privacy and accessibility
- Are warnings placed before risky actions?
- Are secrets and personal information protected?
- Are permissions and access requirements explained?
- Is language clear and inclusive?
- Do links and visual descriptions support accessibility?

## 6. Editorial quality
- Is terminology consistent?
- Are sentences direct and unambiguous?
- Are acronyms expanded on first use?
- Are spelling, punctuation and formatting consistent?
- Are screenshots and diagrams current and labelled?

## 7. Review outcome
Record the document title and version, reviewer and date, critical accuracy issues, usability issues, suggested changes, items requiring technical verification and approval status.

A document should not be marked technically verified merely because it has passed an editorial review. Content accuracy and usability may require separate checks by a subject-matter expert or by testing the documented procedure.
