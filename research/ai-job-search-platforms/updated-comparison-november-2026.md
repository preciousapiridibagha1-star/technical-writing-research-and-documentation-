# AI Job Search Platforms — Updated Research and Comparison

**Research checked:** 7 November 2026  
**Original report reviewed:** *AI Job Search Platforms: Deep Research & Comparison Report 2026*, marked June 2026  
**Platforms in the original report:** Jobright, Sonara, LoopCV and Sorce  
**Research status:** Updated desk research using current product pages, terms and app-store information. No paid accounts were created and no applications were submitted as part of this review.

## Executive summary

The June 2026 report needs a substantial correction before it can be used to make decisions. Some prices and product details have changed, some claims are not supported by evidence shown in the report, and its overall rankings are stronger than the research method allows.

The most important correction for a job seeker in Nigeria is this. The conclusions below are based on public information, not on accounts or applications tested by the researcher:

- **LoopCV** is the clearest candidate to test first from the information currently available. Its official pricing page describes a free plan and says its paid Standard and Premium plans can search for remote jobs anywhere in the world. That does not guarantee that every vacancy accepts applicants from Nigeria; each job must still be checked.
- **Sorce** has a useful free tier according to its own website, but it currently requires an iPhone. Its official pages say there is no Android app yet. The Nigerian App Store lists the app, but this does not prove that every job it shows accepts applicants based in Nigeria.
- **Jobright** offers job matching, resume tools and application help, but its public pages do not give enough clear information to confirm that all features or job listings are available to people applying from Nigeria. Its homepage's job-search location selector is set to the United States by default. Treat Nigeria access as unconfirmed until tested.
- **Sonara** is live again under BOLD, but its own Terms say the site is directed to people within the United States and may not be available elsewhere. That makes it a poor first choice for a Nigeria-based job seeker. Its current public pricing is also not clearly published.

This report does not name a universal "best AI job platform". The right choice depends on country eligibility, the types of jobs being targeted, how much control the user wants over applications, the quality of the job matches and the actual cost.

## 1. Why the original report needed correction

The original report contains several issues that affect its conclusions.

### 1.1 It presents old prices as if they are current

The report is marked June 2026. Prices and plan limits can change. The current LoopCV pricing page now displays prices in euros and lists Basic Looper as free, Standard Looper at €8.99 per month, Premium Looper at €29.99 per month and Done For You at €159.99 per month. These do not match the dollar prices in the June report. **Evidence to check:** LoopCV's live pricing page [L1].

The old Sonara figures are also not safe to repeat as current prices. Sonara's current public pages do not provide a clear public pricing table. A third-party price report is not enough to confirm what a new user in Nigeria would actually be charged. **Evidence to check:** Sonara's current website and Terms [S1, S2].

### 1.2 The Sonara annual-price calculation is inconsistent

The original report lists a rolling price of $23.95 every four weeks and an annual price of $71.40. These cannot both describe the same plan. A four-week billing cycle occurs about 13 times in a year, so $23.95 × 13 is about $311.35, before any taxes or changes. The $71.40 figure should not be described as the annual cost of that rolling plan without separate proof of a different annual offer.

### 1.3 It treats company claims as independently proven results

The original report repeats platform claims about millions of jobs, users, applications, interviews and hires. Those numbers may be useful context, but a company's own marketing figures are not independent proof that the tool works well for every user.

The revised report labels company-published numbers as company claims. They should not be treated as verified outcomes for an individual applicant.

### 1.4 It uses ratings without enough detail

The old report lists Trustpilot and Product Hunt ratings, but does not consistently record the exact profile URL, the date checked, the number of reviews, the rating scale, or the method used to select and interpret reviews. Some figures also conflict within the report.

A review score can change quickly. It can also be affected by who chooses to leave a review. Ratings are therefore shown only when a dated, checkable source is available. They are not used as a substitute for testing the product.

### 1.5 The reliability scores are not properly explained

The old report gives each platform a reliability score and weighted total, but does not explain how the underlying scores were measured or how evidence was collected. A score such as 8.2/10 looks precise, but it is not meaningful if the scoring process cannot be repeated.

The revised report removes those scores. It uses clear strengths, limits and unknowns instead.

### 1.6 It does not properly assess whether a Nigerian applicant can use the service

This is a major gap. A platform can have an attractive free plan but still be unsuitable if its jobs, application systems or terms are limited to another country.

The revised comparison checks public country information where available. Where official sources do not clearly confirm Nigeria support, the report says so instead of guessing.

### 1.7 It treats auto-apply as automatically good

Automatically sending more applications is not always better. A tool may misunderstand a question, apply to a job with the wrong location or work-authorisation rules, or submit a generic answer. A high application count does not prove a high chance of getting an interview.

For important roles, applications should be checked carefully. Never let an AI tool invent qualifications, work experience, visa status, salary history or answers to screening questions.

## 3. Platform notes

### 3.1 Jobright

**What it appears to offer**

Jobright describes itself as an AI job-search assistant. Its public pages describe personalised job matches, resume tailoring, application autofill, a job tracker, networking suggestions and an AI career assistant called Orion. The company currently advertises more than eight million jobs and hundreds of thousands of new listings per day. These are figures published by Jobright, not independently verified counts.

**What needs care**

- Do not assume every feature is included in the free plan.
- Check the plan limits and credit use inside the account before paying.
- The company advertises job matching and application support, but a match score is not proof that an employer will consider an application.
- Confirm whether the specific vacancy accepts applications from Nigeria.
- Review all AI-edited CV content. It must remain truthful.

**Nigeria decision:** The public pages checked do not provide a clear enough answer about full Nigeria support. Treat this as unconfirmed rather than calling it unavailable. **Evidence checked:** Jobright's public website and platform page [J1, J2].

Official website: https://jobright.ai/

### 3.2 Sonara

**What it appears to offer**

Sonara's current website describes a service that finds matching jobs and applies on a user's behalf. Its current Terms identify BOLD LLC as the operator. This is important because the original report describes Sonara's earlier shutdown and later relaunch but does not make the present operating position clear enough.

**What needs care**

- The Terms say the site is directed to people within the United States. They also warn that availability may be limited by country or area.
- The current public website does not provide a clear, complete pricing table that can be relied on for a Nigeria-based buyer.
- Do not rely on the old report's $71.40 annual figure or its application-failure percentage as current facts.
- Confirm the full renewal price, trial conditions, cancellation process and country eligibility before entering payment details.

**Nigeria decision:** Because the Terms are US-focused, this is not a sensible first choice for someone applying from Nigeria unless Sonara confirms otherwise. **Evidence checked:** Sonara Terms [S2].

Official website: https://www.sonara.ai/  
Terms: https://www.sonara.ai/terms-conditions

### 3.3 LoopCV

**What it appears to offer**

LoopCV lets users set job searches, match vacancies to a CV and automate some application activity. Its official pricing page currently lists:

- Basic Looper: free; one job-title search, up to 10 applications or emails per month, three job boards and two AI CV checks.
- Standard Looper: €8.99 per month on the displayed monthly price; up to 20 searches and 100 applications or emails per month.
- Premium Looper: €29.99 per month on the displayed monthly price; up to 50 searches and 300 applications or emails per month.
- Done For You: €159.99 per month on the displayed monthly price; includes an advisory call each week and additional CV support.

Prices, billing periods, taxes and regional charges may change. The price displayed at checkout should be treated as the final current price.

The official pricing page says Standard and Premium can search for remote jobs anywhere in the world. This is more useful for an international job seeker than a service that is clearly limited to US users.

**What needs care**

- "Remote anywhere" does not mean every employer accepts applicants in Nigeria.
- A free plan's monthly application limit is small, so use it to test matching quality and the application process, not to judge the service by volume alone.
- Check the job source, employer, country restrictions and application status.
- Avoid allowing generic AI messages to be sent to recruiters without reviewing them.

**Nigeria decision:** LoopCV is the most practical first test in this four-platform shortlist because its pricing page describes a free entry plan and worldwide remote-job search on paid plans. This is a reason to test it, not proof that it will produce better results. Verify each vacancy's eligibility before applying. **Evidence checked:** LoopCV pricing [L1].

Official pricing: https://www.loopcv.pro/pricing/  
Official website: https://www.loopcv.pro/

### 3.4 Sorce

**What it appears to offer**

Sorce uses a swipe-style interface. Its official FAQ says users upload a CV, set preferences, review matched jobs and swipe right on roles they want to apply for. The AI agent then fills in the application and submits it. The company advertises 40 free swipes per day, including its AI application agent.

Sorce also publishes company-reported figures such as users, swipes, applications and people hired. These should be labelled as company-reported figures, not independent proof of likely results.

**What needs care**

- The official download page says Sorce is currently available on iOS and that Android is not yet available.
- The Nigerian App Store listing shows that the app is listed in Nigeria's store. This does not prove that every job in its database is open to Nigerian applicants.
- The official FAQ says an application may be submitted after the user swipes right. A swipe is therefore an important decision, not just saving a job for later.
- The official guide says unusual application forms may sometimes prevent completion.
- Applications requiring a portfolio, video or a writing sample should be handled manually and checked carefully.

**Nigeria decision:** Worth considering if the user has an iPhone and the app shows suitable international vacancies. It is not a practical option for an Android-only user right now. **Evidence checked:** Sorce's download page, FAQ and Nigerian App Store listing [C1–C3].

Official download information: https://www.sorce.jobs/articles/sorce-app-download  
Official FAQ: https://www.sorce.jobs/articles/sorce-faq  
Nigerian App Store listing: https://apps.apple.com/ng/app/sorce-ai-job-search/id6504584959

## 4. Practical recommendation for a Nigeria-based job seeker

Based on the public information checked on 7 November 2026:

1. **Test LoopCV's free plan first.** Use a small number of carefully chosen searches and inspect the actual jobs it finds. Do not upgrade until it has shown that it can find roles that accept applicants from Nigeria.
2. **Check Jobright's country settings and free access.** If it allows the user to select relevant locations and shows suitable vacancies, compare its matches with jobs found directly on employer websites.
3. **Consider Sorce only if an iPhone is available.** Check the location and work-authorisation requirements of each job before swiping right, because the app says that this action can trigger an application.
4. **Do not pay for Sonara unless its support team confirms Nigeria eligibility and gives the full price and renewal terms.** Its Terms are directed at people in the United States.

This is a cautious order for testing, not a claim that one platform has been proven to produce more interviews.

## 5. A simple test before trusting any platform

Use the same test for each platform:

1. Search for five real jobs that match the user's actual skills and experience.
2. Check whether each employer accepts applicants living in Nigeria.
3. Check whether the role is truly remote or only remote within a named country.
4. Compare the job title, required skills, experience level and salary with the original job advert.
5. Check whether the platform is only finding jobs, filling forms or actually submitting applications.
6. Review every CV change and screening answer.
7. Confirm the application appears on the employer's own careers website or in a genuine confirmation email.
8. Record any failed, duplicate, unsuitable or incorrectly submitted applications.
9. Check the price, renewal date, cancellation method and refund rules before starting a paid plan.

A platform should not be judged only by how many applications it sends. The more useful question is: **How many suitable, accurate applications does it help the user complete?**

## 6. Method and limitations

This is an updated desk review, not a full hands-on product test.

- Official product pages and terms were prioritised for features, pricing, country restrictions and device support.
- Company marketing figures are labelled as company claims.
- Third-party reviews were not used to create numerical reliability scores.
- No platform account was created, no payment was made and no job application was submitted during this review.
- Job availability and country eligibility can vary by employer, vacancy and date.
- Prices can change and may differ by billing period, currency, tax or location.
- The research does not prove that any platform will produce interviews or employment.

The next useful validation step is a controlled free-plan test from the user's own device and location. Record the results before making any paid recommendation.
