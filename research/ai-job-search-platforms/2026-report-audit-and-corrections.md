# Audit of the June 2026 AI Job-Search Platforms Report

**Audit date:** 7 November 2026  
**Original report:** *AI Job Search Platforms — Deep Research & Comparison Report 2026*  
**Platforms covered:** Jobright, Sonara, LoopCV and Sorce  
**Purpose:** Record problems that must be corrected before the report is republished or used to make decisions.

## Overall finding

The original report has a useful idea: compare tools that help people find jobs and send applications. But some of its strongest conclusions are not supported well enough. It mixes company marketing claims, review-site ratings, user complaints and claimed hands-on testing without showing enough evidence for each one.

The report should not keep its old winner rankings or reliability scores. We should rebuild the comparison using information that can be checked, clearly mark what we could not verify, and separate features from actual results.

## Main problems found

### 1. The research method is not clear enough

The report says it used Trustpilot, Product Hunt, Reddit and hands-on testing. It does not give:
- the exact pages or links checked;
- the date each rating or price was recorded;
- how many reviews or discussions were examined;
- how the reviews were selected;
- what hands-on tests were performed, on which account or device;
- what counted as a successful application;
- how the reliability scores were calculated.

**Correction:** Do not say "hands-on testing" unless we have a saved test record. Add a source log and describe the checks that were actually done.

### 2. The reliability scores look more exact than the evidence allows

The report gives scores such as 6/10 and 8.2/10, but does not show enough evidence to explain why one platform received each score. The weighted table also does not clearly explain how the category scores were chosen.

**Correction:** Remove the old scores. Use plain evidence-based labels instead: "feature stated by provider", "listed on official pricing page", "reported by users", "not independently tested" and "not confirmed". A score can be added later only if we create a fair scoring method and apply it consistently.

### 3. Some feature labels are too broad

The comparison uses "auto-apply" as though every service does the same thing. In reality, a tool may:
- find a job;
- recommend a match;
- tailor a CV;
- fill in an application form;
- prepare answers for review;
- submit an application itself;
- track whether the employer received it.

These are different actions. Filling a form is not always the same as submitting it, and a submitted application is not proof that an employer received or reviewed it.

**Correction:** Compare each step separately and say whether the user or the platform performs it.

### 4. Jobright's job-count and user-count claims need dates and attribution

The original report gives figures such as 8 million listings, 400,000 new postings a day and 1.25 million users. These figures may be marketing claims, and the report does not preserve the exact pages or dates that support them. Jobright's own comparison pages now make broader feature claims, including matching, resume and cover-letter tools, referrals and application support. These claims should be attributed to Jobright rather than treated as independently proven results.

**Correction:** Use only figures visible on a dated official page, label them "company-reported", and avoid comparing database sizes unless the companies count active, unique listings in the same way.

### 5. Sonara's status and pricing need to be updated

The old report describes Sonara as relaunched after its earlier shutdown and lists specific prices, including a $2.95 trial and $23.95 every four weeks. Current official terms identify BOLD LLC as the operator and describe an AI service that searches for jobs and applies on the user's behalf. The terms also explain automatic renewal, cancellation and refund conditions. However, the current public pricing could not be confirmed from a public pricing page during this audit.

**Correction:** Do not present the old price as today's confirmed price. State that the current terms name BOLD LLC, explain the renewal and cancellation conditions, and say that the current price must be checked in the sign-up flow before payment. The terms also warn that applications may not be received or processed correctly by employer websites.

### 6. LoopCV's old prices and feature limits are out of date

The current official pricing page lists a free Basic Looper plan with one job-title search, up to ten applications or emails per month, three job boards and two AI CV checks. It lists Standard at €8.99/month, Premium at €29.99/month and Done For You at €159.99/month on the page checked for this report. These do not match the original report's dollar prices and plan limits.

**Correction:** Replace the old pricing table with the current official plan names, currencies and limits. Record the date checked. Do not convert currencies unless the exchange-rate date and method are stated.

### 7. Sorce's mobile availability needs a fresh check

The original report says Sorce is iOS-only and that Android is still coming. The current Google Play listing shows an Android app, while the Apple App Store also lists an iPhone app. Sorce's own download material still describes iOS as its primary app experience, so availability and feature parity should not be treated as identical across platforms.

**Correction:** Say that listings are available on both Google Play and Apple's App Store at the audit date. Do not assume both versions have exactly the same features or user experience.

### 8. The review evidence is too weak for broad reliability claims

The report turns review ratings and selected complaints into claims such as application failure rates of 25–40%, poor matching for 90% of users, refund denials and successful interviews at major companies. These are serious claims. Without links, dates, sample sizes and a clear way of checking them, readers cannot judge whether they are representative.

**Correction:** Remove unsupported percentages and claims of typical user outcomes. If review themes are included later, show the source, date, number of reviews checked and that online reviews are self-selected and may not represent all users.

### 9. The report assumes a US-centred job search

Some platforms and features may be limited by country, device, employer website or job source. The original report does not properly test whether a person living in Nigeria can register, see relevant international roles, use the application feature, pay for the service or apply to employers outside the United States.

**Correction:** Add a separate "Use from Nigeria" check for registration, supported locations, job coverage, currency/payment, device access, and whether the tool can apply to international roles. If a point is not confirmed, say so. Do not assume that a website being accessible means its jobs or application service are available worldwide.

### 10. The final recommendation is too confident

The old report names Sorce as the best overall platform and recommends auto-apply services without enough evidence about location access, application quality, employer receipt or user control.

**Correction:** Do not declare one universal winner. Recommend tools by task, and give a cautious recommendation only after checking whether the service is available and useful in the reader's country.

## What we must not claim

- We must not say that we personally tested an application flow if we did not.
- We must not say an application reached an employer just because a tool says "submitted".
- We must not present company-reported user numbers as independently verified.
- We must not treat online reviews as a scientific survey.
- We must not assume that a US-focused platform is suitable for a job seeker in Nigeria.
- We must not recommend paying for a service before its current price, renewal terms, country support and cancellation process are clear.

## Next step

Rebuild the main report around the reader's real needs: finding suitable roles, tailoring applications, keeping control over what is submitted, international availability (especially from Nigeria), current cost and data/privacy risks. Keep the four original platforms for continuity, but allow the evidence to show that one may not be suitable. Add other platforms only if they materially improve the comparison.


## Final evidence audit — 9 November 2026

The November comparison is more cautious than the June report: it removes unsupported reliability scores, separates company claims from independent evidence and treats Nigerian access as unconfirmed where official sources do not establish it.

One inconsistency remains: the November comparison says Sorce is iOS-only, while this audit records a Google Play listing. These statements must not both be published as current fact. Recheck the official download page and current Google Play and Apple App Store listings on the publication date. Report app listing availability separately from feature parity and country eligibility.

Other publication rules:
- Record the date, currency and billing period for every price; verify the final checkout amount before purchase.
- Do not infer that a vacancy accepts Nigerian applicants merely because a platform can be opened in Nigeria.
- Do not equate a platform's “submitted” status with confirmed employer receipt.
- Do not use review ratings or selected complaints as representative reliability measures without a reproducible sampling method.
- Do not publish universal rankings where the scoring method cannot be repeated.

**Evidence boundary:** This remains public-source desk research. No paid accounts, payments or live applications were tested. The report is not a hands-on evaluation.
