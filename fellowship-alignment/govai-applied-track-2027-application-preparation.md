# GovAI UK Summer Fellowship 2027 — Applied Track Application Preparation

**Research checked:** 7 November 2026  
**Application deadline:** 23:59 UK time, Sunday 22 November 2026  
**Fellowship dates:** 7 June–27 August 2027  
**Location:** London, full-time and in person  
**Status:** Preparation document. This is not an application submission and does not claim eligibility or selection.

## 1. Why the Applied Track is the relevant track to examine

GovAI describes the Applied Track as work on projects beyond traditional research. Its examples include communications, operations, programme and research management, policy engagement, events, and internal products or workflow improvements.

That is a closer match to this portfolio than presenting the applicant as an established AI governance researcher. The portfolio demonstrates early evidence of structured problem analysis, technical communication, system planning, risk identification and careful technology evaluation. It does not yet demonstrate professional AI governance delivery or a record of managing programmes in that field.

The application should therefore make a modest, evidence-led case for potential contribution to applied work. It should not claim experience that the portfolio does not show.

## 2. A defensible applicant profile

**AI systems thinking, technical research and documentation, with an interest in practical AI governance.**

The applicant approaches AI projects by:
- identifying the problem and people affected before choosing a technology;
- examining whether AI is suitable and where simpler approaches may be better;
- defining system responsibilities, permissions and human oversight;
- identifying risks, failure cases and operational limits;
- comparing tools and documenting uncertainty;
- turning a broad idea into a bounded plan that can be reviewed and tested.

This is a description of the work represented in the portfolio, not a claim of professional AI governance employment or deployed systems.

## 4. The application story to build

A credible application narrative can connect three ideas:

1. **Motivation:** Advanced AI is creating important questions about how organisations understand, assess and manage its use. The applicant wants to contribute to the practical work that helps decision-makers respond carefully.
2. **Evidence of approach:** In self-directed projects, the applicant starts with the user problem, sets boundaries, considers human oversight and documents what would need to be tested before implementation.
3. **Contribution and growth:** The applicant is interested in applied work that needs clear communication, organised research, sound judgement and the ability to turn complex ideas into useful plans. The fellowship would provide a chance to develop these abilities alongside people working directly in AI governance.

The applicant should add genuine personal examples and motivations in their own words. Do not invent employment, policy experience, community impact, stakeholder interviews or implementation results.

## 5. Likely strengths to evidence against GovAI's criteria

GovAI says it assesses relevant expertise, quality of work, judgement and team fit.

### Relevant expertise
Use the portfolio to show systems thinking, technical research, documentation, risk awareness and interest in responsible AI use. Explain the connection to applied AI governance without overstating prior field experience.

### Quality of work
Choose a small number of strong examples rather than listing every file. The platform comparison and its correction audit are useful because they show both analysis and willingness to correct weak claims. Vantage is useful for showing how a proposal can include boundaries and human review.

### Judgement
Use concrete decisions:
- keeping human approval before consequential external actions;
- refusing to describe an unverified prototype as working software;
- narrowing Jarvis from a large ecosystem to one proposed customer-enquiry workflow;
- distinguishing official product claims from independent evidence;
- recording what could not be verified.

### Team fit
Be ready to discuss how work is improved through feedback, how uncertainty is communicated and how priorities are chosen. Do not simply say these are strengths; prepare real examples and be candid about areas still being developed.

## 6. Gaps to address before submitting

- Read the GovAI Applied Track page and understand the difference between the Applied and Research tracks.
- Review the portfolio for broken links, inconsistent project names and any wording that accidentally implies implementation.
- Prepare a concise CV that accurately records education, work, independent projects, tools and dates. Do not invent formal roles or qualifications.
- Select two or three portfolio examples and be able to explain the problem, your contribution, decisions, limitations and next steps.
- Prepare for the approximately 20-minute abstract reasoning assessment if invited.
- Keep enough time available for a paid remote work test in January and a remote interview in February if invited.
- Confirm that a full-time, in-person stay in London from 7 June to 27 August 2027 is feasible. GovAI says it can sponsor temporary work visas, but sponsorship is not a guarantee of a visa.

## 8. Programme details and selection process

According to GovAI's current call:
- the first stage is a written application;
- shortlisted applicants complete an approximately 20-minute automated abstract reasoning assessment;
- the second stage is a paid remote work test, expected in January;
- the third stage is a remote interview and reference checks in February;
- final decisions are expected in March;
- the fellowship is full-time and in person in London;
- the salary is £14,000 for the three-month fellowship, with travel support;
- temporary work visa sponsorship is available for successful applicants who need it.

These details can change. Recheck the official call before submitting.

## Final positioning reminder

The strongest case is not that the applicant is already an AI governance expert. It is that the applicant has a clear and honest way of approaching complex AI-related problems, can show the quality of that work, understands the limits of current evidence, and is serious about developing a career that contributes to responsible decisions about advanced AI.
