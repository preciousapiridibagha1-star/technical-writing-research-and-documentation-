## 1. Why the Applied Track is the relevant track to examine

GovAI describes the Applied Track as work on projects beyond traditional research. Its examples include communications, operations, programme and research management, policy engagement, events, and internal products or workflow improvements.

That is a closer match to this portfolio than presenting the applicant as an established AI governance researcher. The portfolio demonstrates early evidence of structured problem analysis, technical communication, system planning, risk identification and careful technology evaluation. It does not yet demonstrate professional AI governance delivery or a record of managing programmes in that field.

The application should therefore make a modest, evidence-led case for potential contribution to applied work. It should not claim experience that the portfolio does not show.

## 2. A defensible applicant profile

**Emerging AI governance practitioner focused on evidence-led research, responsible AI systems thinking and clear technical communication.**

The applicant approaches AI projects by:
- identifying the problem and people affected before choosing a technology;
- examining whether AI is suitable and where simpler approaches may be better;
- defining system responsibilities, permissions and human oversight;
- identifying risks, failure cases and operational limits;
- comparing tools and documenting uncertainty;
- turning a broad idea into a bounded plan that can be reviewed and tested.

This is a description of the work represented in the portfolio, not a claim of professional AI governance employment or deployed systems.

## 4. The application strategy: three pillars

Every answer should make a deliberate contribution to at least one of these pillars. Across the complete application, all three must be visible.

### Pillar 1 — Evidence: what you have actually done

Use two or three strong examples rather than a catalogue of every portfolio project:
- **Vantage Automata:** a self-directed human-supervised workflow concept plus a narrow deterministic local reminder proof of concept with nine recorded passing unit tests. It is not an AI-powered, externally integrated or deployed product.
- **FINOS AI Governance Framework issue #424:** source-based review of G7 AI SBOM guidance, submitted as a public issue discussion comment on 12 November 2026. It demonstrates research, critical analysis and technical communication; it is not a code PR, merged upstream contribution or FINOS endorsement.
- **Research and documentation portfolio:** source evaluation, correction of unsupported claims, clear user-facing explanations, structured documentation and transparent limitations.

For each example use: **Context → Your action → Reasoning and decisions → Verifiable output → Limitation or lesson → Relevance to GovAI.** Be precise about what you personally did.

### Pillar 2 — Motivation: why this work matters to you and why GovAI

The applicant's authentic motivation is not simply an interest in AI tools. It is a concern about how AI systems affect people and how responsibility is retained when organisations use them. The applicant has repeatedly focused on human oversight, investigating systemic causes of discriminatory AI decisions, limiting autonomous permissions and preserving a human authority to pause or suspend a harmful system.

Connect that motivation to GovAI's mission: helping decision-makers navigate the transition to a world with advanced AI. Explain why the Applied Track is attractive: it works on practical organisational projects beyond research alone and combines delivery with expert Q&As, seminars, skills workshops and peer feedback. The applicant wants to build the judgement and practical capability to contribute responsibly over the long term.

Avoid generic claims such as “AI is the future” or “I have always been passionate about AI.” Use the specific concerns and work that genuinely shaped the applicant's interest.

### Pillar 3 — Contribution: what you can bring to the programme

Make a realistic case for the capabilities already evidenced:
- structured source-based research and comparison;
- technical writing and turning complex material into clear explanations;
- workflow and systems thinking, including scope, requirements, boundaries and human approval;
- identifying evidence gaps, uncertainty and risks rather than hiding them;
- self-directed project organisation, documentation and iterative improvement;
- willingness to learn, accept feedback and work within a team's priorities.

Translate these into possible junior-level contributions to applied projects: preparing research and briefing materials, documenting processes, organising information, supporting project coordination, reviewing workflow requirements and communicating technical ideas clearly. Do not claim professional experience in event delivery, fundraising, policy advocacy, operations management or automation engineering unless a real example supports it. State that these are areas in which you can contribute through transferable skills and develop with supervision.

### The central application argument

> I am building a career around helping organisations make responsible decisions about AI. My self-directed work has given me practical experience in source-based research, technical documentation and analysing how AI-enabled workflows should be bounded and overseen. I am particularly concerned with preserving meaningful human authority, identifying risks such as discriminatory outcomes, and ensuring that systems do not gain permissions beyond what is justified. I want to contribute these evidence-led and communication skills to practical AI governance projects while learning from people doing this work directly.

This is a strategy statement, not a finished answer to paste into every form field. Tailor it to the actual question and keep the applicant's natural voice. Never invent employment, policy experience, community impact, stakeholder interviews or implementation results.

## 6. Likely strengths to evidence against GovAI's criteria

GovAI says it assesses relevant expertise, quality of work, judgement and team fit.

### Relevant expertise
Use the portfolio to show systems thinking, technical research, documentation, risk awareness and interest in responsible AI use. Explain the connection to applied AI governance without overstating prior field experience.

### Quality of work
Choose a small number of strong examples rather than listing every file. The platform comparison and its correction audit are useful because they show both analysis and willingness to correct weak claims. Vantage is useful for showing how a proposal can include boundaries and human review.

### Judgement
Use concrete decisions:
- keeping human approval before consequential external actions;
- refusing to describe an unverified prototype as working software;
- narrowing Jarvis from a large ecosystem to one proposed customer-enquiry workflow;
- distinguishing official product claims from independent evidence;
- recording what could not be verified.

### Team fit
Be ready to discuss how work is improved through feedback, how uncertainty is communicated and how priorities are chosen. Do not simply say these are strengths; prepare real examples and be candid about areas still being developed.

## 9. Programme details and selection process

According to GovAI's current call:
- the first stage is a written application;
- shortlisted applicants complete an approximately 20-minute automated abstract reasoning assessment;
- the second stage is a paid remote work test, expected in January;
- the third stage is a remote interview and reference checks in February;
- final decisions are expected in March;
- the fellowship is full-time and in person in London;
- the salary is £14,000 for the three-month fellowship, with travel support;
- temporary work visa sponsorship is available for successful applicants who need it.

These details can change. Recheck the official call before submitting.
