Yes. Mozilla Foundation's official 2027 call for applications explicitly lists **Slum & Rural Health Initiative (Nigeria)** among the five host organisations for its 2027–2028 **Embedded Fellowship** track:

This verifies that SRHIN is named by Mozilla as a host in this call. It does **not** mean SRHIN independently awards the fellowship, guarantees selection, or can collect an application fee. The application route is Mozilla's official Letter of Intent form linked from Mozilla's call page. Do not pay an intermediary or send money to anyone claiming to secure a place.

## 2. Important distinction: Raising p(bloom) versus Embedded

Mozilla describes **Raising p(bloom)** as one of the 2027 focus areas, centred on increasing the likelihood that AI produces broadly beneficial outcomes. However, the official Letter of Intent form presents the focus-area selection under **Track II: Independent Fellows**. Track I applicants instead select an Embedded host organisation and explain why that host is appropriate.

Therefore, this application should not be framed as an Independent Fellowship proposal under the Raising p(bloom) label. The stronger and more accurate route is:

- Apply to **Track I: Embedded Fellow**.
- Select **Slum & Rural Health Initiative** as the host.
- Develop a technically grounded, public-benefit project aligned with SRHIN's confirmed host brief.
- Explain how the project could help AI produce better outcomes for people, without claiming that the project is formally classified by Mozilla as the Raising p(bloom) track.

The thematic overlap is real; the track classification is different.

## 4. Location and remote-work position

SRHIN publicly identifies its campus and some programme activity with **Ibadan, Oyo State, Nigeria**. This is not evidence that the Fellow must relocate to Ibadan.

Mozilla's official FAQ says most 2027 host organisations can accommodate remote Fellows, while advising applicants to check each host's time-zone preferences. It does not publicly confirm, in the FAQ, that SRHIN specifically guarantees remote participation.

Working assumption: remote work may be possible, but this remains **unconfirmed for SRHIN**. Before making a relocation decision, obtain written answers to:
1. Can a Fellow based in Port Harcourt work remotely for the full project?
2. Are any visits to Ibadan or other locations required, and how often?
3. If travel is required, can it be charged to the Fellowship project budget?
4. What working hours and meeting-time expectations apply?

Mozilla's FAQ also says in-person programme activities are not mandatory, although applicants able to attend may be preferred; programme travel and accommodation are covered by Mozilla. This is separate from any host-specific fieldwork or visits.

## 5. Fellowship structure and dates

According to Mozilla's official 2027 call:
- Embedded package: **US$75,000 stipend + US$25,000 project budget** (US$100,000 total).
- Fellowship duration: 12 months, beginning 1 June 2027.
- Letter of Intent deadline: 17 December 2026, 11:59 PM Pacific (UTC−8), equivalent to **18 December 2026, 7:59 AM in Nigeria**.
- Invitations to full application: 16 February 2027.
- Full application deadline: 12 March 2027.
- Final notifications: 30 April 2027.

The stipend is not the same as a guaranteed take-home amount. Mozilla's FAQ says Fellows are responsible for their own tax reporting and that the payment arrangement may vary by region. The applicant must be legally able to receive the funding in the country where they reside and work, and must have a bank account in their own name that can receive US funds.

## 6. Critical eligibility issue: obtain confirmation before heavy preparation

The official Letter of Intent form currently contains a placeholder in the Embedded geographic eligibility question: **"[INSERT]"**. The form also includes a location-based funding gate. Mozilla's FAQ states that an individual must be able to receive funding from a US-based 501(c)(3) organisation and must be eligible to receive the grant/stipend in their country of residence and work.

The public call listing a Nigerian host does not, by itself, prove that every person living in Nigeria is eligible. Nor does the placeholder prove that Nigeria is excluded. The applicant should ask Mozilla to confirm in writing:
- Whether a Nigerian resident based in Port Harcourt is eligible for Track I.
- Whether Nigeria satisfies the Embedded geographic eligibility requirement for SRHIN.
- Whether the applicant can receive the stipend as an individual in Nigeria, and what payment route and documentation are required.

Official contact listed by Mozilla: **fellowships@mozillafoundation.org**. Use the contact address reached from Mozilla's official website, not a third-party agent.

## 8. A sensible project direction to investigate — not a claim about SRHIN's brief

A plausible direction for discussion with the host is a **community-centred assurance and feedback layer for AI-supported mental-health information or screening**.

Potential questions to investigate:
- How can users understand what an AI-supported mental-health tool can and cannot do?
- How can consent, data minimisation, privacy and withdrawal be made understandable?
- How should a tool communicate uncertainty and avoid presenting screening as diagnosis?
- How can culturally and linguistically appropriate content be reviewed by local experts and people with lived experience?
- When should the system stop and refer a person to a qualified human?
- How can errors, complaints, bias and user feedback be documented and acted upon?
- What evidence would demonstrate that the tool improves access or user understanding without creating new risks?

This is only a research hypothesis. It must be changed if SRHIN's official host brief identifies a different problem. It is not appropriate to build a mental-health chatbot or screening model independently and then claim it is clinically safe.

## 9. Portfolio evidence plan

Before the Letter of Intent, prioritise evidence that can genuinely be produced and reviewed:

1. **Host and problem research:** a source-linked brief distinguishing verified SRHIN programmes from questions that need host confirmation.
2. **Responsible AI requirements:** a requirements document covering intended users, exclusions, consent, privacy, language, accessibility, uncertainty, escalation and human oversight.
3. **Risk and safeguarding register:** risks, affected people, likelihood/severity, mitigations, owners and unresolved questions. Clinical risks must be reviewed by qualified professionals.
4. **System and data-flow diagrams:** clearly labelled as proposed designs, not deployed architecture.
5. **Evaluation plan:** define what would be measured, how user understanding and accessibility could be assessed, and what would count as evidence of benefit or harm.
6. **Technical prototype or reproducible demonstration:** only if feasible, with synthetic or public non-sensitive data; no real patient data and no diagnostic claims.
7. **Open-source documentation contribution:** one genuine, scoped contribution to an existing project, recorded with its issue, pull request and actual review outcome. Do not invent or self-award a merged contribution.
8. **Community engagement plan:** explain how SRHIN and relevant community members would guide the work. Do not claim interviews or consent before they occur.
