# Master Portfolio — Opportunity Evidence Map

**Portfolio model:** One coherent evidence base for the original eight-opportunity plan. Tarbell was later removed at the user's direction, leaving seven active targets.  
**Anchor lens:** NetHope Humanitarian AI Research Fellowship — the eighth opportunity in the original plan.  
**Purpose:** Explain how the portfolio's connected research, systems analysis, governance and documentation evidence can be selected for each opportunity without creating separate professional identities.

## The central explanation

The portfolio follows one working method:

**Understand a technology problem and the people affected → research the evidence → analyse the system and its implications → develop a proportionate recommendation or design → document the reasoning, risks and limitations.**

The projects are not unrelated. Vantage and Jarvis explore AI system design and oversight. Research briefs examine evidence and technology choices. Governance work considers accountability, privacy and human authority. Technical writing makes the analysis usable. Open-source contributions provide opportunities to apply these skills in public projects.

## Why NetHope is the anchor lens

The NetHope Humanitarian AI Research Fellowship focuses the portfolio on a consequential public-interest question: how humanitarian and nonprofit organisations can evaluate, adopt and govern AI responsibly.

The current evidence provides a foundation, not proof of humanitarian expertise:

| Portfolio evidence | Relevance to humanitarian AI research | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Research methodology and source-based comparisons | Assessing tool claims, evidence quality, suitability and uncertainty | Desk research; not field evaluation or systematic review |
| Vantage Automata | Analysing human approval, permissions, action boundaries and accountability | Narrow local deterministic proof of concept; not an AI deployment |
| Jarvis AI | Analysing proposed AI-enabled organisational workflows and oversight | Concept and documentation; no verified integration |
| AI recruitment governance case study | Examining consequential AI decisions, privacy, fairness and human review | Developing hypothetical case study; not an audit |
| Nigeria-focused research and digital-policy work | Context-sensitive analysis of digital systems and public-interest issues | Public-source research; not humanitarian fieldwork |
| Technical documentation | Communicating findings, requirements, limits and practical guidance | Writing samples; not commissioned humanitarian deliverables |

The next high-value evidence addition is a rigorous humanitarian AI adoption/governance case study based on credible public sources. It should identify a bounded use context, stakeholders, potential benefits, risks, institutional constraints, human oversight, data protection, procurement/accountability questions and evidence gaps. It must not invent interviews, field observations, organisational endorsement or impact.

## Current strengths and evidence gaps

### The portfolio can currently demonstrate
- Self-directed research and source checking.
- Systems analysis and documentation of proposed AI workflows.
- A narrow local deterministic prototype with recorded unit-test results.
- Awareness of human oversight, privacy, permissions and accountability.
- Technical writing, documentation review and information architecture.
- One real submitted open-source documentation pull request.

### The portfolio cannot currently claim
- Humanitarian field research, aid-sector employment or a partnership with a humanitarian organisation.
- Interviews, consultation or co-design with affected communities.
- A deployed Vantage, Jarvis or MindCommons AI system.
- A verified AI integration or production engineering experience.
- A merged open-source contribution until maintainers merge a PR.
- Measured community outcomes, professional legal advice or independent external audit.

## Development priorities

1. Build a source-grounded humanitarian AI adoption/governance case study as the anchor research sample.
2. Continue improving the shared research method: transparent source selection, claim checking, limitations and reproducible reasoning.
3. Complete the planned Internet Society learning and a focused internet-governance issue brief.
4. Continue narrow, genuine open-source documentation contributions and record actual review outcomes.
5. Preserve the distinction between concept, desk research, prototype, submitted PR and verified external outcome.
6. Tailor each application by selecting evidence from this same portfolio; do not create separate identities or inflate programme fit.

## Application rule

The portfolio stays unified. For each opportunity, choose the most relevant evidence, explain the connection in the applicant's own words and disclose the limits. A strong fit is demonstrated through accurate work and sound judgement, not by claiming experience that has not been gained.
