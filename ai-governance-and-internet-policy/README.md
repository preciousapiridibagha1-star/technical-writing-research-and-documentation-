# AI Governance & Internet Policy Portfolio — Build Plan

**Owner:** Precious Apiri Dibagha  
**Status:** Active learning and evidence-building project  
**Started:** 8 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Build practical, reviewable evidence of knowledge and skills relevant to applied work in AI governance and internet policy.

> This repository records learning, analysis and deliverables as they are completed. A planned activity is not evidence of completed work. Every factual claim must be sourced, every limitation must be visible, and no policy expertise or implementation experience should be overstated.

## Intended capability areas

1. **AI governance and policy understanding** — understand the institutions, actors, policy instruments, risks and practical governance questions surrounding advanced AI.
2. **Internet governance and digital rights** — understand how internet policy is developed and how rights, safety, access, privacy and security interact.
3. **Research and analytical writing** — formulate bounded questions, evaluate primary sources, compare evidence, distinguish findings from interpretation and communicate uncertainty.
4. **Technical documentation and communication** — explain technical systems, workflows, risks and recommendations to non-specialist and specialist audiences.
5. **Project planning and implementation** — define objectives, stakeholders, scope, deliverables, dependencies, risks, measures of success and realistic execution plans.

Open-source contribution and polished portfolio samples are later-stage outputs. They will be selected after the first five capability areas have been developed, so that contributions are informed rather than superficial.

## Working method

For each topic, use the same cycle:

1. Define a specific question and why it matters.
2. Identify authoritative primary sources and relevant independent analysis.
3. Read and extract evidence; maintain a source log.
4. Explain the issue in plain language.
5. Analyse trade-offs, affected groups, implementation constraints and uncertainty.
6. Produce a practical output (brief, explainer, requirements note, risk register or project plan).
7. Review for accuracy, balance, clarity and unsupported claims.
8. Record feedback and revisions.

## Quality and integrity rules

- Prefer laws, official policy documents, regulator publications, standards and original research for primary claims.
- Record publication dates, access dates, jurisdiction and the scope of each source.
- Distinguish established facts, contested claims, analysis, recommendations and open questions.
- Do not present a proposal as a deployed solution or a study plan as completed research.
- Do not fabricate stakeholder engagement, interviews, user testing, project outcomes or open-source contributions.
- Do not copy substantial copyrighted material; summarise and cite appropriately.
- Use AI tools as research and drafting support only where permitted, and independently verify the output.
- Keep the work grounded in actual governance problems and decision-making needs, not generic descriptions of AI.
