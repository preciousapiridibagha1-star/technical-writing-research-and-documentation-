# Research Sources

**Last checked:** 9 November 2026

This list separates official sources from third-party reproductions. Public information is not a substitute for SRHIN's internal technical documentation or direct community consultation.

## Official Mozilla sources

1. **Mozilla Foundation — 2027 Call for Applications**  
   https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/what-we-do/grantmaking/fellowship/2027-call-for-applications/  
   Confirms the 2027–2028 fellowship tracks, host organisations including SRHIN, funding structure and application timeline.

2. **Mozilla Foundation — 2027 Fellowship FAQs**  
   https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/what-we-do/grantmaking/fellowship/2027-call-for-applications/faqs/  
   Explains the difference between Embedded and Independent Fellows and clarifies that Embedded Fellows collaborate with a host on an independent project aligned with the host's mission.

3. **Mozilla Foundation — Full 2027–2028 RFP PDF**  
   https://assets.mofoprod.net/network/documents/PUBLIC_2027_2028_Fellowship_RFP-2.pdf  
   The full RFP is linked from the official call. The SRHIN-specific section must be checked directly before finalising application claims.

## Official SRHIN and GENSCORE sources

4. **SRHIN — Our Work**  
   https://srhin.org/our-work/  
   Public overview of SRHIN's programme areas.

5. **GENSCORE — Platform**  
   https://www.genscore.io/  
   Public description of GENSCORE, its culturally grounded mental-health screening work and Gencurate data-curation process.

6. **GENSCORE — Governance and Ethics**  
   https://www.genscore.io/governance.html  
   Public description of governance oversight, consent, de-identification, access controls, ethics and fairness safeguards.

7. **SRHIN — ALPHA Center**  
   https://srhin.org/alpha-center/  
   Public description of the AI-for-low-resource public-health initiative.

## Third-party reproduction of the host challenge

8. **Rekoja — Mozilla Fellowship 2027 guide**  
   https://rekoja.com/find/mozilla-fellowship/guide  
   Reproduces the SRHIN host challenge as a call to explore community-owned tools for mental-health data governance, consent, privacy and benefit-sharing, with pilots involving young people, people with lived experience and community health workers, toward a mental-health data commons. This is a third-party reproduction, not the primary source. Verify wording against the official RFP.

## Evidence handling

- Official programme and platform pages establish what their publishers publicly describe; they do not independently prove every performance or impact claim.
- Third-party summaries are discovery aids and should not replace the official RFP.
- No internal SRHIN documents, APIs, data models or operating procedures have been reviewed for this concept.
- No community interviews or participatory research have been conducted for this proposal.
- All product gaps remain hypotheses until validated with SRHIN and affected communities.
