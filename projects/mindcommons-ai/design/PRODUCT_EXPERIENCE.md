# Product Experience — Community Data-Use Accountability Ledger

## Product promise

Make one approved mental-health data use easier for authorised community stewards to trace: what was approved, what conditions apply, what has changed and what community-agreed commitments remain.

This is not a general-purpose dashboard or research portal.

## Three work areas

### 1. Approved uses
A searchable, permission-controlled list of approved data-use records. Each record shows purpose, conditions, decision authority, date, review date and source links. It references authoritative records and does not expose participant data.

### 2. Change review
A focused submission and review journey for a material change to an existing approved use. It shows the original decision, the proposed change, relevant conditions, missing information, assigned human authority and final human decision.

### 3. Community commitments
A small register of commitments explicitly agreed by the relevant community and authorised parties, linked to the approved use and decision. It shows owner, due date, progress and evidence.

## AI assistant

One integrated assistant appears contextually beside the current record or request. It should not occupy half the screen or become the main product.

It can explain a condition, help complete a change request, identify missing information, compare the request with approved conditions and summarise follow-up. It must cite the records used and make uncertainty visible.

AI never approves, grants access, changes consent or decides benefits.

## User experience principles

- One record should connect the use, its source decision, conditions, changes and commitments.
- Keep the number of fields to the minimum agreed with SRHIN.
- No new consent journey if Gencurate or another approved process already covers it.
- No participant-level health information in the ledger.
- Show who has authority and link to the authoritative decision.
- Distinguish proposed, under review, approved, rejected, expired and closed states.
- A user must be able to complete each task without chatting with AI.
- Mobile-friendly, low-bandwidth and plain-language.

## Validation

Before visual design is finalised, confirm actual user roles, decision rights, data fields, access levels, existing system boundaries, language/accessibility requirements and whether a separate workspace would reduce rather than increase work.
