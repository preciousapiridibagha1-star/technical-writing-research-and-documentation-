# Product Definition

## 1. Working product name

**MindCommons AI**  
*A community-facing AI stewardship interface for responsible mental-health data use.*

The name is provisional and subject to later brand checks.

## 2. Product decision

The first product concept was too broad. SRHIN publicly describes Gencurate consent and data-curation workflows, a Lived Experience Working Group with governance responsibilities, a Data, Ethics & Safety Monitoring Board, a Human Oversight Charter, escalation procedures, reporting and community-governance resources.

MindCommons AI must not recreate those structures.

The narrower product hypothesis is a **community-facing data-use stewardship and accountability workspace** that could help connect existing governance decisions to later data-use proposals, review conditions, transparent records and agreed community benefits.

This is not yet a confirmed product requirement. SRHIN may already have internal tools that perform these functions.

## 3. The problem we are investigating

When mental-health data is considered for a new research purpose, a new collaborator, model development or another later use, can the relevant community stewards easily understand:

- What use is being proposed?
- Which consent conditions and community decisions apply?
- Which authorised body must review it?
- What conditions were attached to the decision?
- What happened after approval?
- What benefits were agreed, who is responsible and what progress has been made?

The research question is whether this information is currently connected and accessible enough for community oversight, not whether SRHIN has governance.

## 4. What the product could be

A focused web application or interface that works alongside existing approved systems, if a genuine need and safe integration path are confirmed.

Potential users:
- Community stewards and lived-experience representatives.
- Authorised governance and ethics reviewers.
- Researchers preparing a new or changed data-use proposal.
- Participants, but only for information and choices not already handled by an existing consent system.

Potential capabilities:
1. **Data-use register:** plain-language view of approved purposes, conditions, status and review dates.
2. **Use-change requests:** structured proposals for new collaborators, new purposes, AI/model development or other material changes.
3. **Governance trace:** links each proposal to the applicable community decision, ethics review, consent constraints and recorded rationale.
4. **Community accountability:** privacy-preserving view of approved uses, conditions and agreed benefit commitments.
5. **AI stewardship assistant:** explains approved governance records, checks proposals for missing information, compares them with documented conditions and prepares reviewer summaries.

These capabilities are candidates for validation, not commitments to build every module.

## 5. AI is central, but bounded

The AI assistant is embedded in the workspace rather than being the whole product.

It could:
- Explain an approved governance decision in plain language.
- Help a researcher prepare a complete change-of-use request.
- Compare a proposal with documented conditions and flag missing evidence.
- Summarise a request for LEWG or authorised reviewers.
- Answer role-authorised questions about a decision's status and review history.
- Prepare a plain-language summary of benefit commitments and progress.

It must not:
- Grant data access or approve a new use.
- decide whether consent is legally or ethically sufficient.
- Override LEWG, ethics boards or community authority.
- invent rules or infer permission from vague language.
- expose participant-level or sensitive information to unauthorised users.
- decide what benefit-sharing is fair.

Application permissions, structured policy checks and authorised humans control consequential actions.

## 6. How it differs from GENSCORE and Gencurate

| Existing public SRHIN work | MindCommons AI should not duplicate | Possible complementary question |
|---|---|---|
| Gencurate: consent checked before collection, annotation and clinician review | A new audio collection, consent capture or clinical curation system | Is there a separate need to track later purpose changes and approved reuse conditions? |
| LEWG and Human Oversight Charter | A replacement lived-experience group or governance charter | Do existing community decisions need a more accessible operational record across subsequent data uses? |
| Data, Ethics & Safety Monitoring Board, escalation protocols and SOPs | A new ethics board or replacement oversight process | Is there a shared workflow that connects a proposal to the correct reviewer and records conditions? |
| Monthly dashboards, quarterly reports and annual audits (as publicly described) | A generic KPI dashboard or audit system | Is there a community-facing view of specific approved uses and benefit commitments that is not already available? |
| SRHIN Knowledge Hub governance resources | Another set of static governance templates | Is there a need to apply approved rules consistently to real proposals and keep a traceable record? |

## 7. Proposed experience

The product should be a focused dashboard-first workspace, not a generic chatbot.

- **Community Stewardship Dashboard:** pending use-change proposals, decisions requiring follow-up, conditions due for review and benefit commitments.
- **Researcher Request Workspace:** submit a new or changed data-use proposal with purpose, data category, collaborators, AI use, duration and supporting approvals.
- **Governance Review:** authorised reviewers see the relevant rules, prior decisions, missing evidence and AI-prepared summaries.
- **Transparency View:** role-appropriate explanations of approved uses, conditions and benefit progress without revealing private records.
- **AI Assistant:** context-aware support throughout the workflow.

A participant-facing consent centre is not assumed for the first scope because Gencurate already publicly describes consent-before-collection. It should only be included if SRHIN identifies a separate participant need not covered by current systems.

## 8. Success criteria to validate

A useful solution should:
- Reduce the effort needed to trace a proposal to applicable governance decisions.
- Make missing evidence and review responsibilities clearer.
- Help community stewards understand what has been approved and under what conditions.
- Improve visibility of follow-up actions and agreed benefits.
- Avoid duplicating existing records or adding unnecessary administrative work.
- Preserve confidentiality and existing decision authority.

Measures and baselines must be agreed with SRHIN and community partners; none are claimed yet.

## 9. Relationship to SRHIN

MindCommons AI is an independent project concept being explored in alignment with SRHIN's mission. It is not commissioned, approved, endorsed or deployed by SRHIN.

No integration with GENSCORE, Gencurate or internal systems is assumed. The host and community partners must first confirm the gap, intended users, data boundaries, existing workflow and technical feasibility.

## 10. Product boundaries

This is not:
- A mental-health screening, diagnosis or treatment tool.
- A replacement for GENSCORE or Gencurate.
- A replacement for LEWG, ethics oversight or SRHIN's governance charter.
- A new consent-capture tool by default.
- A public database of mental-health records.
- An autonomous AI access-control or ethics decision-maker.
- A blockchain or token-governance system.

## 11. Current status

Research and product definition only. No prototype, production system, integration, community consultation or host approval is claimed. The next step is to validate whether the narrower stewardship workflow is genuinely missing or burdensome. If it is already handled effectively, the concept must be revised or stopped.
