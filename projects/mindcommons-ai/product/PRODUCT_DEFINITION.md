# Product Definition

## 1. Product name

**MindCommons AI**  
*An AI-powered platform for community-governed mental-health data commons.*

The name is provisional and subject to later brand checks.

## 2. Product vision

Help communities have a meaningful voice and practical tools to govern how mental-health data contributed by their members is used in research and AI development.

## 3. What are we proposing?

MindCommons AI is a web application that combines role-based workspaces with an integrated AI assistant.

It supports the people involved in the data-use journey:

- Participants understand and manage their individual permissions.
- Researchers prepare and track requests to use data.
- Governance reviewers assess requests against approved rules.
- Community representatives participate in oversight and benefit-sharing.
- Administrators manage accounts, roles and system settings.

The platform is intended to support a community-governed mental-health data commons. It is not simply a database containing health records. A data commons requires agreed community governance, clear stewardship, rules for access and use, and arrangements for shared benefit.

## 4. Why include AI?

AI is a central assistance layer, not a decorative chatbot.

One coordinated AI service can provide different role-aware capabilities:

| AI capability | What it helps with |
|---|---|
| Participant Assistant | Explains consent, data-use purposes and participant options in plain language and supported local languages. |
| Research Assistant | Guides researchers through request forms, checks for missing information and summarises submissions. |
| Governance Copilot | Compares requests with approved policies and permission records, flags risks or conflicts and prepares a review summary. |
| Transparency Assistant | Answers authorised questions about request status, decisions and audit records using approved information. |
| Community Benefit Assistant | Summarises agreed benefit commitments, responsibilities and delivery progress. |

These are task-specific capabilities of one coordinated AI layer. They do not require five independent AI models.

## 5. What AI may and may not do

**AI may:**
- Explain approved policies and consent text.
- Translate or simplify information, with a way to check the approved wording.
- Guide users through forms.
- Identify missing fields or inconsistencies.
- Compare a request with a structured set of approved rules.
- Summarise evidence for human reviewers.
- Search and summarise records the user is authorised to see.
- Draft reports about decisions and benefit commitments.

**AI must not:**
- Give clinical diagnoses or mental-health treatment advice.
- Decide whether a person should participate in research.
- silently change a participant's consent.
- Grant access to sensitive data on its own.
- override community governance decisions or ethics approvals.
- invent policy, consent terms or benefit commitments.
- reveal records beyond the user's permissions.
- make final decisions about benefit distribution or community representation.

## 6. Product structure

The application has five user-facing areas:

1. **Participant Centre** — consent information, permissions, data-use explanations and support.
2. **Researcher Workspace** — research purpose, data-use requests, supporting information and request status.
3. **Governance & Community Workspace** — review queues, policy checks, human decisions, community review and governance records.
4. **Transparency & Benefits** — authorised activity history, public-safe summaries and community benefit commitments.
5. **AI Assistant** — a conversational assistant available in context throughout the application, subject to the user's role and permissions.

## 7. Intended value

- Make complex data-use information easier to understand.
- Give participants clearer visibility into their choices.
- Help researchers submit complete and better-structured requests.
- Help reviewers apply approved rules consistently.
- Make governance decisions easier to explain and audit.
- Help communities monitor agreed benefit-sharing commitments.
- Support responsible use of community-contributed data in research and AI development.

## 8. Relationship to SRHIN

The product is intended to align with SRHIN's public work in community mental health, digital health and AI. It should complement existing programmes and governance arrangements rather than replace them.

No integration with GENSCORE, Gencurate, BraveHeart or other SRHIN systems is assumed. The need, data flows, APIs, ownership, hosting and security arrangements must be confirmed with SRHIN.

## 9. Product boundaries

MindCommons AI is not a clinical service, mental-health screening tool, electronic medical record, generic chatbot or replacement for existing SRHIN platforms. It is a proposed governance and community oversight application focused on mental-health data and its use in research and AI development.

## 10. Product success

The product should ultimately be judged by whether intended community members can understand and influence data-use governance, whether requests are handled transparently and consistently, whether privacy is protected, and whether agreed community benefits can be monitored. These outcomes require community-led evaluation; software usage counts alone are not enough.
