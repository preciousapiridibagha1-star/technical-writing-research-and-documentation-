# Fellowship Proposal: Focused MVP and Project Case

## 1. The larger vision

The long-term vision is a modular AI-powered organisational intelligence platform. It would help organisations bring programme information together, manage implementation, monitor performance, identify data-quality problems and prepare evidence-based reports.

The broader product architecture is intended to support eight areas over time:

1. Public health.
2. Health organisation programmes.
3. Education.
4. Youth development.
5. Humanitarian projects.
6. Community development.
7. Environmental programmes.
8. Research and advocacy.

This is the product vision, not the scope of the first funded build.

## 2. The problem we want to investigate

Many organisations manage programme information across spreadsheets, documents, forms and separate applications. This can make it difficult to maintain a consistent picture of programme delivery, identify missing information, compare progress with targets and prepare reports that can be traced back to source records.

This is a problem hypothesis, not a claim that every organisation experiences the same difficulty. The project must validate the problem with prospective users and understand what they already use.

## 3. Who the first project is for

The initial intended users are programme teams and monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL/M&E) staff in:

- Public health programmes.
- Health organisations delivering non-clinical programmes and service-improvement work.
- Education programmes.

The project is designed around organisational programme information. It is not a clinical system and will not manage patient care.

No host organisation, pilot partner or user commitment is confirmed at this stage. The project should identify and engage suitable organisations during the discovery and validation phase before claiming an institutional need or partnership.

## 4. Why a focused tool may be useful

The project will test whether a shared workspace can make existing programme information easier to review and use, without requiring an organisation to replace every tool it already has.

The proposed value to investigate is:

- Less manual consolidation of programme records.
- Clearer visibility of activities, indicators, targets and progress.
- Earlier identification of missing or inconsistent data.
- Faster preparation of draft reports.
- Better traceability from a reported result to the source record.
- A configurable experience for different programme sectors.

These are intended benefits to measure, not outcomes already achieved.

## 5. What the fellowship MVP will include

The proposed MVP is deliberately narrower than the full vision. It will focus on one shared platform foundation and three sector configurations: Public Health, Health Organisation Programmes and Education.

### Capability A — Data Hub

- Import a limited set of CSV or spreadsheet formats.
- Preview records before importing.
- Map common fields.
- Flag missing, inconsistent or possible duplicate records.
- Keep a reference to the source file and import.
- Require a person to review and confirm uncertain records.

### Capability B — Programme Monitoring

- Create and view programmes and activities.
- Define a small set of indicators, units and targets.
- Record progress by reporting period.
- Calculate totals and target-versus-actual values using deterministic application logic.
- Flag overdue activities and missing updates.

### Capability C — AI Intelligence and Reporting

- Ask questions about information available in the workspace.
- Summarise programme activity and progress.
- Explain data-quality warnings.
- Draft a programme progress report.
- Link important statements to source records and show where evidence is missing.

AI-generated content will be a draft for human review. AI will not invent missing values or calculate official indicator totals. Calculations will be performed by validated application logic.

## 6. What will not be included in the first MVP

- All eight sector modules.
- A complete enterprise platform.
- A hospital electronic medical record or clinical decision-support system.
- Patient diagnosis, treatment or sensitive patient case management.
- Full replacement of existing organisational systems.
- Complex accounting, payments or donor/fundraising management.
- Every possible external integration.
- Enterprise-scale deployment or advanced offline mobile functionality.

## 7. How the project will be delivered independently

The project will be independently led. The project lead will be responsible for problem definition, research, product direction, requirements, coordination, documentation and evaluation.

The project budget will be used, subject to fellowship rules and approved cost categories, to engage experienced technical collaborators for work that requires specialist skills. Likely support includes backend and frontend engineering, AI integration, data architecture, security/privacy review and testing.

The project lead will not claim to personally provide every specialist engineering function. The work will be planned and reviewed with qualified collaborators, with clear deliverables and staged acceptance criteria.

## 8. How the project budget will be used

The proposed project budget will support a bounded MVP rather than the entire long-term platform. Spending will be refined after user discovery, technical scoping and written estimates from collaborators.

Priority areas are:

1. User discovery and workflow validation.
2. Backend and data model implementation.
3. A usable frontend for the agreed workflows.
4. Limited, evidence-grounded AI features.
5. Security and privacy review.
6. Testing, pilot feedback and documentation.
7. Hosting and essential software services.

The earlier illustrative $25,000 allocation is a planning exercise only. It is not a vendor quote or proof that the full platform can be built for that amount. The final budget must match the fellowship's current rules and the actual MVP requirements.

## 9. How we will evaluate the MVP

The project should assess whether intended users can:

- Import and review programme data with fewer avoidable errors.
- Find programme activities and indicator progress in one workspace.
- Identify missing updates and inconsistent records.
- Produce a draft report with traceable supporting evidence.
- Understand and correct AI-generated content before it is used.

Possible measures include import error rates, time required to prepare a report, completeness of indicator updates, proportion of report statements linked to evidence and user feedback on usability. Baselines and targets should be set with pilot organisations rather than invented in advance.

## 10. What needs to be validated before final submission

- Which specific organisations and staff experience the problem most acutely?
- What tools and workarounds do they currently use?
- Which data formats and workflows are common?
- What information are they willing and authorised to process?
- Which parts of the proposed workflow would genuinely save time or improve oversight?
- What existing products already meet these needs?
- What is the smallest useful MVP that can be delivered within the approved budget?
- Which technical collaborators and pilot organisations can credibly support delivery?

## 11. Positioning statement

We propose to independently lead the development and validation of a focused, AI-supported organisational intelligence MVP for public health, health organisation and education programmes. The project will bring selected programme records into a shared workspace, support monitoring against indicators and targets, identify data-quality gaps and help teams prepare evidence-based draft reports. We will use the project budget to engage experienced technical collaborators for specialist engineering, AI, security and testing work. The MVP will test a defined part of a larger modular vision; it will not be presented as the completed long-term platform.
