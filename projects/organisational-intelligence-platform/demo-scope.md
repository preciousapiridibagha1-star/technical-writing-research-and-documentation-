# Demonstration Scope

## 1. Purpose

Build a small, working demonstration that communicates the product concept and proves a few important user journeys.

This is not a commitment to build the complete platform or a production-ready MVP before the fellowship presentation.

## 2. Initial sector scenarios

The demonstration will include three sample configurations:

1. Public Health.
2. Health Organisation Programmes.
3. Education.

Use fictional organisations, synthetic records and clearly labelled sample data. Do not use real patient, student or beneficiary information.

## 3. Three shared demonstration capabilities

### Capability 1: Data Hub
Demonstrate:
- Loading or uploading sample CSV/Excel information.
- Previewing records.
- Mapping basic fields.
- Flagging missing or inconsistent values.
- Reviewing and confirming imported information.

### Capability 2: Programme Monitoring
Demonstrate:
- Creating or selecting a sample programme.
- Viewing activities.
- Defining indicators and targets.
- Recording actual progress.
- Calculating target-versus-actual performance.
- Identifying overdue activities or missing updates.

### Capability 3: AI Intelligence and Reporting
Demonstrate:
- Asking questions about the sample programme data.
- Summarising activities and progress.
- Identifying missing information.
- Explaining a performance gap.
- Preparing a draft report with links or references to supporting sample records.

## 4. Example demonstration scenarios

### Public health
A fictional community health initiative uploads outreach activity records and a monitoring spreadsheet. The platform shows planned versus completed outreach, communities reached and missing updates.

### Health organisation
A fictional health organisation reviews facility-support activities, training sessions and service-improvement milestones. The platform summarises implementation progress without using patient-identifiable data.

### Education
A fictional education programme uploads school-support and teacher-training records. The platform shows schools supported, training delivered and progress against programme targets.

## 5. What is in scope

- A simple web interface.
- Three sector scenarios using shared workflows.
- Synthetic sample data.
- Basic file import or preloaded sample files.
- A limited programme and indicator data model.
- Deterministic calculations.
- A limited AI question-answering and report-drafting workflow, if feasible.
- Clear demonstration documentation.

## 6. What is out of scope

- Full implementation of all eight sector modules.
- Production deployment for real organisations.
- Real patient records or clinical workflows.
- Clinical diagnosis or treatment recommendations.
- Full integration with every external application.
- Complex accounting or donor management.
- Advanced offline mobile data collection.
- Enterprise-scale analytics.
- Complete automated workflow orchestration.
- A claim of a fully finished commercial MVP.

## 7. Demonstration success criteria

A reviewer should be able to understand:
- The organisational problem being explored.
- How the platform brings programme information together.
- How the same core works across three sector configurations.
- How users monitor activities and indicators.
- How AI helps interpret available information.
- How generated reports remain tied to source records.
- What has actually been built and what remains proposed.

## 8. Honest presentation language

Describe the work as an early working demonstration or proof of concept of a broader modular platform.

Do not describe the complete platform as built. Distinguish implemented features, simulated features, design proposals and future development.
